I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Mississippi, with an expected graduation date of Spring 2028. My research interests are in health economics, public economics, and applied microeconomics. I study how public policies affect health, treatment access, household behavior, and individual well-being. My current research examines involuntary civil commitment laws for substance use disorders, the effects of Québec’s low-fee universal childcare reform on female employment and intimate partner violence, and the design of sugar-sweetened beverage taxes. I use applied microeconometric methods and administrative and survey data to evaluate the effects of these policies.

Before beginning my Ph.D., I earned an M.A. in Economics from Central Michigan University and a B.A. in Economics from Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology.
